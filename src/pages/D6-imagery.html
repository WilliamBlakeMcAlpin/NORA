D6 · Imagery and graphic style

Caught, never sold.

Two treatments. Black-and-white for the object, almost always. Low-key color for people, rarely. Darkness dominates both.

NORA · D6 Imagery01
Sample: crop of NORA's amber macro, converted
Primary · black and white

The product as an object.

  • High contrast. True blacks. Let shadows fill in.
  • One light. Hard or soft, never a lit-from-everywhere studio.
  • Negative space. The frame takes up no more than a third of the image.
  • Surface stays real. No retouching of acetate grain, metal or engraving.
  • Converted in post. Shoot color RAW, convert to black and white. Keep a color master.
  • Macro is welcome in either treatment: acetate, metal, engraving, leather.

Secondary · low-key color · sparingly

People, in the dark.

  • One practical light in the scene: a lamp, a bar light, a window. Warm.
  • Darkness dominates. Most of the image falls to black.
  • Candid. Faces turned away, in profile or in silhouette. Nobody looks at the lens.
  • Nothing staged. If it looks lit for a shoot, reshoot.
  • Unisex. Cast and pose without gender cues. The same direction for everyone.
  • At most one in three campaign images uses color.

For the photographer

Technical brief.

  • Shoot RAW, color. Black-and-white conversion in post.
  • Deliver at 4:5 (feed), 9:16 (stories) and 3:2 (web), cropped from one master
  • Leave room: the hairline and labels need the lower third of a 4:5 frame clear
  • No visible logos, signs or posters from other brands anywhere in frame
  • Clean dust, keep texture. No skin smoothing, no material retouching.
  • File names: style_shot_treatment, e.g. blake_05_bw.tif

Graphic devices · approved

Three devices. One per layout.

The Prism

The case profile as a hairline triangle. A marker for sections, highlights and the box seal. Never filled, never holds the logo.

OPEN: proportions from case sketches

—/333

The Numeral

N° —/333 before launch. Real numbers only on the card and the frame. "N° 001/333" only as a labeled sample.

The Hairline

The lockup rule as the one line in a layout, at 61.8% of the height. Headlines rest on it; labels hang below.

Leopard is not a device. It is a material accent: at most about 5% of a layout, and only from NORA's own acetate once photographed.
